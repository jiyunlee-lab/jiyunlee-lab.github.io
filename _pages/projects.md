        <h3>
          Understanding the Role of Citizen Engagement and Multidirectional
          Information Exchange in Community Resilience to Wildfires
        </h3>

        <p class="project-card__short-title">
          Citizen Engagement for Wildfire Resilience
        </p>

        <p class="project-card__summary">
          This project establishes a dynamic and interactive approach to
          wildfire risk assessment and management that promotes citizen
          engagement, multidirectional information exchange, and collective
          decision-making among community members and local governments.
        </p>

        <div class="project-meta">
          <div class="project-meta__item">

        <div class="project-themes" aria-label="Research themes">
          <span>Natural Hazard Modeling</span>

          <span>Risk Assessment</span>
          <span>Community Resilience</span>
          <span>AI and Machine Learning</span>
        </div>

        <details class="project-details">
          <summary>View project details</summary>

          <div class="project-details__body">

            <section>
              <h4>Research Problem</h4>
              <p>
                Wildfires increasingly threaten people, the built environment,
                and ecosystems as climate conditions, human activities at the
                wildland-urban interface, and land-use patterns continue to
                change. Existing wildfire risk-management strategies often
                rely on static, outdated, and coarse-scale risk estimates.
                They also provide limited opportunities for sustained citizen
                engagement, even though individual mitigation decisions can
                affect neighboring properties and the broader community.
                Limited information exchange between governments and citizens
                creates a barrier to informed and coordinated wildfire-risk
                reduction.
              </p>
            </section>

            <section>
              <h4>SHAPE Lab Contributions</h4>
              <ul>
                <li>
                  Characterize interdependent wildfire-mitigation decisions
                  between governments and citizens using evolutionary game theory.
                </li>
                <li>
                  Develop a dynamic wildfire risk-assessment model that
                  incorporates ongoing data collection and processing.
                </li>
                <li>
                  Examine how citizen engagement and multidirectional
                  information exchange influence individual and collective
                  mitigation decisions.
                </li>
                <li>
                  Integrate decision and risk models into a prototype
                  next-generation Community Wildfire Protection Plan platform.
                </li>
                <li>
                  Evaluate the technical feasibility and cost-effectiveness of
                  the prototype platform while incorporating user feedback.
                </li>
                <li>
                  Translate project findings into educational courses,
                  practitioner training, and public outreach materials.
                </li>
              </ul>
            </section>

            <section>
              <h4>Expected Impact</h4>
              <p>
                The project seeks to advance wildfire planning from traditional
                static assessments toward a dynamic and interactive process.
                The resulting approach will help governments and citizens
                exchange information, coordinate mitigation actions, and engage
                in collective, risk-informed decision-making. By strengthening
                collaboration between local governments and residents, the
                project can help expand responsibility for wildfire-risk
                reduction from emergency personnel to the broader community.
              </p>
            </section>

        <details class="project-details">
          <summary>View project details</summary>

          <div class="project-details__body">

            <section>
              <h4>Research Problem</h4>
              <p>
                Wildfire-related power outages can significantly affect
                people, businesses, and communities in different ways. This
                project brings together key partners, including utility
                companies, ratepayers, regulators, local agencies, and health
                and energy organizations, through a community collaborative.
                We study wildfire-related outages, develop actionable
                solutions, and establish a more effective and balanced
                resilience planning framework.
              </p>
            </section>

            <section>
              <h4>SHAPE Lab Contributions</h4>
              <ul>
                <li>
                  Model wildfire ignitions across diverse landscapes,
                  community settings, and ignition sources.
                </li>
                <li>
                  Build an advanced wildfire simulator for regional-scale
                  risk assessment.
                </li>
                <li>
                  Create a cluster-based active-learning scenario reduction
                  framework for probabilistic wildfire risk assessment.
                </li>
                <li>
                  Predict wildfire-related power outages using satellite imagery.
                </li>
                <li>
                  Evaluate community-centric solutions using an interactive
                  dashboard currently under development, which will display
                  the spatial distribution of benefits, costs, and burdens
                  associated with different resource-allocation choices across
                  the service areas of our utility partners.
                </li>
              </ul>
            </section>

            <section>
              <h4>Broader Project Collaboration</h4>
              <p>
                The broader multidisciplinary project team contributes
                complementary expertise in wildfire modeling, power grid
                resilience planning, public health, and community engagement.
              </p>
            </section>

            <section>
              <h4>Expected Impact</h4>
              <p>
                The project will help utility and community partners compare
                resilience strategies and understand how the benefits, costs,
                and burdens associated with different resource-allocation
                decisions are distributed across utility service areas. The
                resulting framework will support technically sound and
                community-informed planning for wildfire-related power outages.
              </p>
            </section>

        <h3>
          Advancing Community Wildfire Preparedness through AI-Driven
          Personalized Evacuation Coordination Plans
        </h3>

        <p class="project-card__short-title">
          EVAC-Advisor: AI-Driven Wildfire Evacuation Planning
        </p>

        <p class="project-card__summary">
          This project develops EVAC-Advisor, a customizable, human-centered,
          LLM-based system that helps residents and emergency managers
          co-design personalized wildfire evacuation plans while balancing
          individual household constraints with broader community safety goals.
        </p>

        <div class="project-meta">
          <div class="project-meta__item">
            <span class="project-meta__label">Funding Agency</span>
            <span>PacTrans Region 10 University Transportation Center</span>

              Travis Paveglio, Co-Principal Investigator

          </div>

          <div class="project-meta__item">
            <span class="project-meta__label">SHAPE Lab Researchers</span>
            <span>Yihan Chen and Abdel-Aziz Sanad</span>
          </div>
        </div>

        <div class="project-themes" aria-label="Research themes">
          <span>Natural Hazard Modeling</span>
          <span>Human Decision-Making</span>
          <span>Community Resilience</span>
          <span>AI and Machine Learning</span>
        </div>

        <details class="project-details">
          <summary>View project details</summary>

          <div class="project-details__body">

            <section>
              <h4>Research Problem</h4>
              <p>
                Wildfire evacuations are becoming increasingly difficult as
                fires become faster and less predictable. Existing evacuation
                guidance often provides standardized instructions without
                accounting for household-specific needs, road capacity,
                visitor populations, transportation limitations, livestock,
                mobility challenges, or traffic congestion. As a result,
                current approaches do not adequately balance individual
                constraints with broader community safety goals. This project
                addresses that gap through a customizable, LLM-based
                co-planning tool that grounds personalized recommendations in
                local wildfire and transportation data and iteratively balances
                them with network capacity and community-level safety.
              </p>
            </section>

            <section>
              <h4>SHAPE Lab Contributions</h4>
              <ul>
                <li>
                  Develop an LLM-driven interface that helps residents create
                  personalized evacuation plans based on their household needs
                  and constraints.
                </li>
                <li>
                  Use retrieval-augmented generation to ground AI
                  recommendations in local road networks, parcel information,
                  wildfire data, refuge areas, and emergency-planning protocols.
                </li>
                <li>
                  Adapt EVAC-Advisor responses to different levels of user
                  trust, technical knowledge, and skepticism toward
                  AI-generated guidance.
                </li>
                <li>
                  Develop a simulation-based optimization engine that balances
                  personalized departure times, routes, destinations, and
                  shared-mobility needs with community-level evacuation goals.
                </li>
              </ul>
            </section>

            <section>
              <h4>Broader Project Collaboration</h4>
              <p>
                The multidisciplinary project team combines wildfire modeling,
                transportation simulation, optimization, artificial
                intelligence, emergency planning, and social-science methods.
                Focus groups and cognitive interviews with residents, fire
                professionals, emergency managers, land-use planners, and
                other local stakeholders will support the co-development and
                evaluation of EVAC-Advisor.
              </p>
            </section>

            <section>
              <h4>Expected Impact</h4>
              <p>
                EVAC-Advisor will help residents and emergency managers
                establish personalized and coordinated evacuation plans before
                wildfire emergencies. By balancing household needs with
                network capacity, shared mobility, and clearance time, the
                system aims to improve community evacuation efficiency and
                safety.
              </p>
            </section>

          </div>
        </details>

      </div>
    </article>
